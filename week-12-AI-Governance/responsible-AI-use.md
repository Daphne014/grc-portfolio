# Responsible AI Use Guideline for Employees

**Scenario:** Small professional services organization (accounting firm, ~40 employees)

## 1. Purpose of AI Use

Employees may use approved AI tools to work more efficiently, such as drafting documents and supporting research. AI assists staff; it does not replace professional judgment. Every AI-generated output must be reviewed by an employee before it is used or shared.

## 2. Approved and Prohibited AI Use

**Approved**
- Drafting emails, reports, or meeting notes, reviewed before finalizing
- Summarizing non-confidential documents
- Supporting research on non-sensitive topics

**Prohibited**
- Entering client data, financial records, or other confidential information into public or unapproved AI tools
- Letting AI make final decisions about clients or business operations without human review
- Using AI-generated content without checking it for accuracy

## 3. Data Handling Rules

Client records, financial details, employee personal data, and other confidential business information must never be entered into an unapproved AI tool. A simple test: if the information would cause harm becoming public, it doesn't go into AI. This protects client trust and reduces exposure risk.

## 4. Bias and Fairness Awareness

AI can sound confident while being incomplete, inaccurate, or shaped by bias in its training data. Staff should not assume AI output is correct by default. Every response should be checked for accuracy and fairness before use.

## 5. Accountability and Escalation

Employees stay responsible for work completed with AI assistance. If an AI tool's use is unclear, or its output seems wrong or involves sensitive data, staff should pause and check with their manager before continuing.
