## Workplace Safety Checklist

I work in a customer-facing role and use CRM systems daily, so data protection is something I think about practically, not just theoretically. Here is the checklist I put together for my work environment:

- **No personal devices or personal email for work data.** Keeping that separation is a basic but important data protection habit.
- **MFA enabled on all work systems**, adding an extra layer of protection beyond just a password.
- **Screen lock set to 30 seconds.** When I step away from my desk, my screen locks automatically so no one can access the data I am working on.
- **A password manager** to generate strong, unique, random passwords across my work accounts and keep my credentials encrypted.
- **Report suspicious activity to IT.** Whether it is an email from an unrecognised source asking me to click a link, or an unexpected approval request on my authenticator app, I check in with the IT team rather than ignore it or handle it alone.

## Personal Safety Checklist

This one felt more personal to build because I had to be honest about my own habits. Here is what mine looks like:

- No guessable passwords, no reusing the same password across accounts.
- Auto-lock screen set to 1 minute on personal devices.
- Consistent OS updates. Not just for new features; updates patch known vulnerabilities that attackers can exploit if left unaddressed.
- MFA on email and social media accounts.
- Pause before clicking any link that looks off. I slow down, hover over it, and confirm it actually leads where it claims to before clicking anything.
- A password manager for personal accounts to keep passwords encrypted and out of my head.
- Account recovery options set up for my cloud accounts, including a backup email address and a recovery phone number, so I have a way back in if something gets compromised.
- Work accounts and personal accounts kept completely separate.
- Periodic review of which third-party apps have access to my Google account. This one surprised me when I actually looked.

> I am already practising several of these and will be building on them as the program progresses.

## Week 2 Takeaway

While working on this assignment, I took time to audit my own Google account security settings. Seeing exactly which apps had access and what was or was not turned on was a wake-up call in the best way. That hands-on audit is my biggest takeaway from Week 2 and honestly the thing I will remember longest from this exercise.
