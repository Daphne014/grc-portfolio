After reviewing the Cyber Roles & Career Paths document from SCWIST, I identified two tracks
that align closely with my background and where I want to grow: Policy &
Governance as my primary focus, with Compliance & Frameworks as the foundational
layer I want to understand deeply first.

---

## Primary Interest: Policy & Governance

**Target roles:** Governance, Risk & Compliance (GRC) Analyst | Security Policy Coordinator

My career to date has been built around operating within structured,
policy-driven environments, even when cybersecurity wasn't the explicit focus.

At Leeds Teaching Hospitals NHS Trust, I worked within one of the most
compliance-heavy sectors in the world. Data protection, procedural adherence,
and documentation accuracy were non-negotiable. At LASEPA, a government
environmental agency, I supported operations requiring strict governance and
accountability frameworks. In telecommunications at Teleperformance Canada and
Sky UK, I worked within regulated environments with defined escalation
procedures, data handling policies, and service standards.

What these roles have in common: the ability to translate policy into practice,
understand organizational risk, and support teams in maintaining standards.
That's the core of GRC and Security Policy work. I didn't carry the
cybersecurity title, but I was already doing governance thinking.

---

## Foundational Layer: Compliance & Frameworks

**Target roles:** Compliance Assistant | Privacy Analyst

Governance work doesn't exist in a vacuum. It depends on a working knowledge of
the compliance frameworks that define what organizations must do. Compliance is
the rulebook; governance is ensuring the organization follows it. I can't do one
well without the other.

Frameworks like NIST, ISO 27001, and PIPEDA are the language of compliance
roles. My background in healthcare (one of the most strictly regulated sectors
for data protection) gives me practical intuition for what compliance looks and
feels like in real organizations. The Privacy Analyst path is one I want to
understand deeply. It bridges data protection, individual rights, and
organizational accountability: themes I encountered across every role in my
career.
