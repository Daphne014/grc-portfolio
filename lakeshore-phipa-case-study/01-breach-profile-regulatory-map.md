# Breach Profile and Regulatory Map
**Lakeshore Health Technologies | GRC Compliance Project | Daphne Obadan**

A real Ontario clinic got investigated by the IPC for how it got rid of old patient files. This project takes that case, PHIPA Decision 266 (IPC, November 22, 2024), and uses it to ask: what would the same failures look like at Lakeshore Health Technologies, a fictional cloud platform managing PHI for GTA family clinics? The clinic's problem was physical. Lakeshore's risks are digital. The legal obligations are identical.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Organization** | Multi-disciplinary health clinic, Ontario |
| **What happened** | Patient records hand-torn instead of shredded, left in a recycling bin, retrieved by a member of the public |
| **Breach period** | June to August 2022 |
| **Patients affected** | 482 |
| **Clinic aware of investigation** | March 3, 2023 |
| **Patients notified** | December 2023 and February 15, 2024 |
| **Gap** | Over 9 months between clinic awareness and patient notification |
| **Sections violated** | PHIPA s.10(1), s.10(2), s.12(1), s.13(1) |
| **Outcome** | No formal order issued; clinic implemented written policies and training after the fact |

**What actually happened.** The office manager was clearing space and started hand-tearing old massage therapy records instead of shredding them, because the shredder noise was disturbing patients in session. The torn records went into a recycling bin, cleaners picked them up, and they ended up in a public dumpster. Someone found them, photographed them, and called the IPC. The clinic had no written policy at the time. Just a verbal understanding that records should be shredded. That understanding clearly wasn't enough.

**Why Lakeshore sits in the same frame.** Clinics and health tech platforms both sit under PHIPA, either as health information custodians or as agents acting on their behalf. The obligations around secure handling, written policies, and breach notification apply regardless of whether the records are physical or digital. That's the connection. The scenario is different, the legal exposure isn't.

**Laws that apply to Lakeshore.**

PHIPA (Ontario) is the primary law here. It governs how health information custodians and their agents collect, store, and dispose of PHI. Lakeshore holds PHI on behalf of its clinic clients, which means it carries direct obligations under the Act.

PIPEDA (Federal) sits alongside PHIPA for private-sector organizations handling personal information commercially. Lakeshore falls here too. Its breach notification requirements aren't a replacement for PHIPA's, they run in parallel.

CPPA, the law that will eventually replace PIPEDA, isn't in force yet but it tightens accountability and breach notification requirements further. Worth building toward now rather than scrambling to catch up later.
