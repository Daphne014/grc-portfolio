# Week 12 Notes: AI Governance & Responsible AI Use

## Acceptable AI Use

In real organizations, AI use follows a pattern: it is approved, not ad hoc. Sensitive data is restricted. Human review is required. Decisions get documented.

**Acceptable use looks like:**
- Drafting summaries, with review before anything is finalized
- Identifying trends in data
- Assisting investigations, not concluding them

**Unacceptable use looks like:**
- Uploading client data into unapproved AI tools
- Letting AI make final decisions on its own
- Using AI outputs without verifying them first

## What AI Governance Means

AI governance answers five questions:
- Where can AI be used?
- Who approves its use?
- What data is allowed?
- How is it monitored?
- How are decisions reviewed?

AI governance is not a separate discipline. It extends risk management, compliance, and ethics into a new category of tool.

## Class Exercise: Is This AI Use Acceptable?

**Scenario 1:** An employee pastes customer data into a public AI chatbot to summarize it.
**Unacceptable.** Data exposure risk, no approval, no accountability.

**Scenario 2:** A security analyst uses AI to draft an incident summary, then reviews and edits it.
**Acceptable.** Human oversight, no sensitive data exposed, AI used as assistance rather than a decision-maker.

The difference between the two scenarios isn't the tool. It's whether sensitive data left an approved boundary, and whether a human stood behind the result before it went anywhere.
