## What is Governance?

Governance is how an organization makes decisions and holds people accountable. In cybersecurity, it answers three questions:

- Who is responsible for security?
- What rules must we follow?
- How do we know those rules are working?

Without governance, security depends on individuals making good decisions in the moment. With it, expectations are clear, roles are defined, and the organization can prove it's managing risk intentionally — which matters for audits, insurance, and regulatory compliance.

---

## Policy, Procedure, Technical Control

These three layers work together. Confusing them is one of the most common mistakes in GRC.

| Layer | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy** | Sets the rule — what must happen and why | "All staff must use multi-factor authentication" |
| **Procedure** | Explains how to follow the rule, step by step | "When onboarding a new employee, IT enables MFA and sends setup instructions" |
| **Technical control** | Enforces the rule automatically | "The system blocks login without MFA" |

A policy without a procedure is just a statement. A procedure without a technical control relies entirely on people doing the right thing. All three together is how security actually holds.

---

## Why Organizations Care About Policies

Policies are not just internal documents. They directly affect:

- **Cyber insurance** — insurers require documented policies and evidence of enforcement before issuing coverage
- **Regulatory compliance** — laws like PIPEDA require written policies, proof they were communicated, and evidence they are followed
- **Audits** — policies become the evidence an auditor checks against. No policy means no proof.

---

## Who Creates Policies?

Policy development is collaborative. It typically involves:

- GRC and Compliance teams — drafting and maintaining
- Legal and HR — reviewing for legal exposure and staff obligations
- IT and Security — confirming technical feasibility
- Leadership — approving and owning the policy

This is where non-technical professionals contribute directly to cybersecurity outcomes.

---

## Key Terms

| Term | Plain English |
|---|---|
| **Governance** | The system that decides how security decisions get made and who is accountable |
| **Policy** | A formal written rule setting behavioral expectations across an organization |
| **Procedure** | Step-by-step instructions for carrying out a policy consistently |
| **Technical control** | A system setting or tool that enforces a rule automatically |
| **GRC** | Governance, Risk, and Compliance — the function that keeps policies current and mapped to requirements |
| **Due diligence** | Documented proof that an organization took reasonable steps to manage risk |
| **Audit evidence** | Records, policies, and logs that prove compliance during an external review |
