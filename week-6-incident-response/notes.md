## What Is an Incident?

A cybersecurity incident is any event that threatens the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of information or systems. Not every incident is a cyberattack — human error, vendor failures, and misconfigurations count too.

The key distinction: a security **event** is something that happens. An **incident** is an event that requires a response.

---

## Incident Types and CIA Impact

| Incident Type | What It Is | CIA Affected |
|---|---|---|
| Phishing / Social Engineering | Deceptive communication designed to steal credentials or data | Confidentiality |
| Malware / Ransomware | Malicious software that disrupts, encrypts, or exfiltrates data | Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability |
| Data Breach | Unauthorized access to sensitive information | Confidentiality |
| Data Exposure (Accidental) | Sensitive data made accessible without intent | Confidentiality |
| Insider Threat | Harm caused by someone with legitimate access | Confidentiality, Integrity |
| System Outage / Availability Incident | Systems unavailable due to cyber or operational causes | Availability |
| Third-Party / Vendor Incident | Organization affected through a compromised vendor | Confidentiality |

> **Note:** Not all outages are cyberattacks  but many are still cyber incidents and require a documented response.

## The Incident Response Lifecycle

Incident response follows a structured cycle. Each phase matters — skipping steps creates gaps that get exploited again.

**1. Preparation**
Everything that exists before an incident occurs: response plans, reporting channels, defined roles, contact lists. GRC Assistants and Program Coordinators often help build and maintain these.

**2. Detection and Identification**
How an incident is discovered. This often starts with a person noticing something — a suspicious email, unusual account activity, a lost device — not a tool.

**3. Containment**
Stopping the problem from spreading. Actions include disabling accounts, removing access, or taking systems temporarily offline. The mindset: contain first, investigate later.

**4. Eradication and Recovery**
Fixing the root cause and restoring normal operations. This includes removing malware, resetting credentials, restoring data, and validating systems before bringing them back online.

**5. Lessons Learned**
Reviewing what happened, what worked, and what did not. Outputs include updated policies, improved training, and stronger controls. This phase is what prevents the same incident from recurring.

## Who Is Involved in Incident Response?

Incident response is cross-functional by design. A response team typically includes:

- IT and Security
- Privacy and Legal
- HR
- Communications
- Leadership
- Third-party vendors (where relevant)

Entry-level roles contribute through coordination, documentation, and communication — not deep technical analysis. This is where non-technical professionals have a direct impact.

---

## What Good Reporting Looks Like

Early reporting reduces damage, even when you are not certain something is an incident. A good incident report captures:

- What happened
- When it happened
- Who discovered it
- What systems or data may be affected

**What not to do:** ignore it, attempt to resolve it quietly, or delay escalation.

---

## Incident Management: What Exists

No specialist tools are required to understand incident management. Common tools include:

- Incident logs
- Ticketing systems
- Secure communication channels
- Breach notification templates
- Evidence tracking documents

Entry-level tasks typically include logging incidents, tracking timelines, documenting actions taken, and supporting communications.
