# Week 6 Homework: Incident Response Plan
## Community Food Bank — Small Nonprofit

**Program:** SCWIST Cybersecurity Skills for Success — 2026 Cohort
**Prepared by:** Daphne Obadan

---

This plan outlines how a small nonprofit food bank with 5 core staff and rotating volunteers should prepare for, respond to, and document a cybersecurity incident. Volunteers handle physical distribution only and do not have access to donor records or financial systems.

---

## Data at Risk

- Donor names, email addresses, and giving history
- Client intake records including household size and dietary needs
- Supplier contracts and procurement pricing
- Staff and volunteer personal information

**Primary CIA impact: Confidentiality.** Donor and supplier data would be exposed to unauthorized parties. Integrity follows because records could be altered or deleted during or after the incident.

---

## Incident Types Most Likely to Occur

| Incident Type | Scenario | CIA Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Phishing / Business Email Compromise | Spoofed supplier invoice results in a fraudulent payment | Confidentiality, Integrity |
| Accidental Data Exposure | Donor folder shared with someone who does not need access | Confidentiality |
| Third-Party / Vendor Incident | Contracted grocery supplier breached, exposing procurement data | Confidentiality |
| Insider Access Issue | Departing staff or volunteer retains system access after role ends | Confidentiality, Integrity |

---

## How Incidents Are Reported

- Staff report to the Admin and Data Officer or Operations Coordinator by phone or in person
- Volunteers report to the Volunteer Coordinator, who escalates immediately
- Do not use email to report if email itself may be compromised
- Every report must capture: what happened, when, what systems or data may be involved, and who else may be affected

---

## Who Is Notified and In What Order

- Admin and Data Officer — first point of contact
- Operations Coordinator — assesses operational impact
- Executive Director — authorizes containment and all external communications
- Legal or Privacy Advisor — engaged when PIPEDA breach notification obligations may apply
- Affected vendors or donors — notified only after Executive Director approves

---

## Initial Containment Steps

- Do not click, forward, or reply to a suspicious email, preserve it as evidence
- Remove incorrect file or folder access immediately, before investigating how it happened
- Suspend data sharing with any vendor under active breach investigation
- Revoke system access for an insider risk before alerting the individual
- Contact the bank immediately if a fraudulent payment may have been made

---

## Documentation Requirements

The Admin and Data Officer logs every incident. Each entry must record:

- Date and time discovered and reported, and by whom
- Incident type and systems or data affected
- Containment actions taken, by whom, and when
- Notifications made internally and externally
- Evidence preserved, resolution, and lessons learned

The Executive Director reviews and signs off before any incident is closed.

---

## Leadership Involvement

- Authorizes all external communications including donor and vendor notifications
- Approves containment actions that affect operations
- Engages legal counsel when PIPEDA obligations may apply
- Signs off on the incident log and lessons learned
- Reports to the board if there is reputational or financial impact
